GEO / AEO · AI Search Analytics

AI Visibility Auditor: Measuring Brand Share-of-Voice in AI Search

PythonOpenAI APILLM-as-JudgeStreamlitGEO / AEO
Problem: Buyers increasingly ask AI "who's the best provider for…" — a new channel where being uncited loses pipeline that never shows up in analytics. No standard tool measures it.
What I built: A company-agnostic tool that asks AI the real questions buyers ask, then scores how often a brand is recommended, how prominently, and against whom — a measurable Share-of-Voice score plus a ranked fix-list. Swap one config file to audit any business.
60 AI answers scored · 20 buyer questions · config-driven for any brand
GitHub repo ↗ Live tool available on request
The Method

One config file generates ~20 intent-weighted buyer questions. Each is asked multiple times in live-search mode, then a second AI model judges every answer — was the brand named, at what rank, with what sentiment, which competitors, which sources. Deterministic scoring turns those reports into a weighted Visibility Score. Separating extraction from scoring keeps every number auditable.

Sample Audit — a regional MSP

Strong on branded questions (cited ~100%, positive) but largely absent on high-intent discovery questions like "best provider in [city]" — the exact questions a new prospect asks. The pattern: strong AI reputation, weak AI discovery — a specific, fixable gap rather than a brand problem.

Built to Scale

Fully config-driven — one file swaps the tool from one business to any other, including sub-brands or executive profiles. Roadmap: trend tracking over time, scheduled audits, and more engines (Gemini, Perplexity).

GA4 Ecommerce Analytics

Diagnosing a 90.3% Cart Abandonment Rate Across Channels & Devices

GA4Looker StudioAttributionFunnel Analysis
Problem: $526K in revenue across 319K sessions — but 90.3% of users who added to cart never bought. Traffic was healthy; conversion was broken.
Key insight: The leak wasn't checkout — it was the product page (75.5% of viewers never added to cart). Desktop converted 3.5x better than mobile, and data-driven attribution showed Email and Organic Shopping were badly undervalued.
$526K revenue · 3.5x desktop/mobile gap · 90-day audit
Open live dashboard ↗
Analysis

Conducted a full GA4 audit of the Google Merchandise Store demo account across a 90-day period. Built a funnel exploration from view_item → add_to_cart → begin_checkout → purchase with device-level and channel-level breakdowns. Compared last-click vs. data-driven attribution models to identify misattributed channel credit. Pulled traffic acquisition data across 10 channels analyzing sessions, engagement rate, session key event rate, and revenue side by side.

Key Findings

Direct traffic dominated sessions (65.6%) but had the lowest engagement rate (21.68%) and worst key event rate (11.71%) — high volume, low quality. Organic Shopping had just 0.24% of sessions but a 74.87% key event rate — the highest converting channel by far. Paid Search generated $46K in revenue from only 2.43% of sessions, the highest revenue-per-session of any channel. Data-driven attribution revealed Email (+9.92%) and Cross-network (+10.66%) were systematically undercredited under last-click.

Recommendations

Prioritize mobile UX improvements — specifically product pages and checkout flow on mobile, where a 1% conversion lift would represent significant revenue given mobile's session share. Shift budget reporting to data-driven attribution before making channel investment decisions. Protect Email and Organic Shopping budgets — cutting them based on last-click data would be a costly mistake.

Lead Generation & Event Marketing

Live Activation & Lead Capture: Total Solar Eclipse Campaign

Event MarketingLead GenerationActivation
Problem: The 2024 eclipse drew thousands to Cotton Bowl Stadium — a captive audience with no plan to convert foot traffic into leads.
What I built: A selfie station — scan a QR code, get your branded eclipse photo emailed instantly, with every capture feeding an automated welcome sequence. Concept to execution, fully in-house.
350+ qualified leads · 6 hours · $0 design cost
The Mechanism

Designed a selfie station at Cotton Bowl Stadium during the 2024 total solar eclipse — a rare captive audience of thousands with nowhere to go. Attendees scanned a custom QR code which instantly delivered their branded photo to their email, embedding a brand introduction alongside it. Concept, design, and execution handled entirely in-house at zero cost.

The Pipeline

Every email capture fed directly into an automated welcome sequence — turning a one-time event interaction into the start of a lifecycle journey. The QR loop meant no manual data entry, no friction, and instant delivery that made the opt-in feel like a reward rather than a form.

Impact

350+ qualified leads captured in 6 hours at $0 design cost. Demonstrated that a well-designed activation loop can outperform paid lead generation — the leads were self-selected, high-intent, and already engaged with the brand before the welcome email arrived.
